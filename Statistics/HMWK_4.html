Ex. 1 – Welford’s Recurrence Formulas

Derive the recurrences for the sum of squares (SS) and the variance, following Knuth’s approach for the mean, and write the code (core logic: about 3 lines).

Step 1: Knuth’s recurrence for the mean

With Mk = (x₁+…+xk)/k we have k·Mk = (k−1)·Mk−1 + xk, hence:

Mk = Mk−1 + (xk − Mk−1) / k

Define the innovation δk = xk − Mk−1. Then Mk = Mk−1 + δk/k and xk − Mk = δk·(k−1)/k.

Step 2: Recurrence for the sum of squares SSk = Σ (xi − Mk)²

Use the shortcut SSk = Σ xi² − k·Mk², and subtract the same expression for k−1:

SSk − SSk−1 = xk² − k·Mk² + (k−1)·Mk−1²

Insert Mk = Mk−1 + δ/k (δ = δk):

= xk² − k·(Mk−1 + δ/k)² + (k−1)·Mk−1²
= xk² − Mk−1² − 2·Mk−1·δ − δ²/k

Since xk² − Mk−1² = δ·(δ + 2·Mk−1), the cross terms cancel:

SSk − SSk−1 = δ² − δ²/k = δ²·(k−1)/k = δk · (xk − Mk)
SSk = SSk−1 + (xk − Mk−1)(xk − Mk), with SS₀ = 0

Step 3: Recurrence for the variance

The unbiased sample variance is sk² = SSk/(k−1) for k ≥ 2. From Step 2, SSk = (k−2)·sk−1² + δ²·(k−1)/k. Dividing by (k−1):

sk² = ((k−2)/(k−1)) · sk−1² + (xk − Mk−1)² / k, k ≥ 2

The population (biased) version is simply σ̂k² = SSk/k. Both need only one pass and O(1) memory, and avoid the cancellation errors of Σx² − k·M².

Step 4: Code (Python) — core logic in 3 lines

M, SS = 0.0, 0.0 for k, x in enumerate(xs, start=1): d = x - M # δ_k M += d / k # mean SS += d * (x - M) # sum of squares var = SS / (k - 1) # unbiased sample variance

The three lines inside the loop (d, M, SS) are the whole recurrence; the same code is used in the simulation below.

Step 4: Conclusions

  • Sampling mean is unbiased. The average of the sampling means lands on μ for every n: E[x̄] = μ.
  • Sampling variance needs n−1. Dividing SS by n−1 gives an average close to σ² (unbiased). Dividing by n underestimates σ² by the factor (n−1)/n, which is clearly visible for small n.
  • Why this matters in inferential statistics. μ and σ² are unknown in practice and only one trajectory is available. These results show that x̄ and s² are valid estimators of the unknown parameters; the spread of x̄ (standard error σ/√n) is what underlies confidence intervals and hypothesis tests.
  • Effect of n. Increasing n does not change the centre of the estimates (still μ and σ²), but their variability shrinks: SD of the sampling means falls like σ/√n, and the SD of the sampling variances falls at a similar 1/√n rate. Larger n gives more reliable single estimates (consistency).

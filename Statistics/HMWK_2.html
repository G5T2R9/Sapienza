3. Standards, Primitives & Conceptual Distinctions

A. Role of the 6 Standards and Cryptographic Primitives

BIP-39 (2013)128-bit Entropy → 12 Words → 512-bit SeedConverts random bits into human-readable backup words and stretches them via PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512 (2048 rounds) into a binary wallet seed.

4. Historical Context & Architecture Discussion

A. Chronological Timeline

  • 1977 — RSA: First practical public-key cryptosystem; introduced asymmetric private/public key pairs.
  • 2001 — SHA-2 / SHA-512: Standardized by NIST; used inside BIP-39 (PBKDF2) and BIP-32 (HMAC-SHA512) for key stretching and derivation.
  • 2012 — BIP-32: Introduced Hierarchical Deterministic (HD) wallets for Bitcoin (single master seed → key tree).
  • 2013 — BIP-39: Standardized 12/24-word mnemonic phrases for human-friendly seed backups.
  • 2014 — BIP-44: Defined standard multi-currency HD derivation paths (e.g., m/44'/60'/0'/0/0 for Ethereum).
  • 2015 — SHA-3 (based on Keccak): Standardized by NIST (FIPS 202) as a sponge-based alternative to SHA-2.
  • 2016 — EIP-55: Introduced backward-compatible mixed-case checksum encoding for 20-byte Ethereum addresses.

B. Distinction: SHA-2 / SHA-512 vs. Keccak-256 / SHA-3

SHA-2 (SHA-256 / SHA-512) uses the classic Merkle-Damgård construction based on Davies-Meyer compression blocks. Keccak / SHA-3 uses a completely different architecture called the Sponge Construction (absorb & squeeze phases with the 1600-bit Keccak-f permutation), making it immune to length-extension attacks.
Crucial Ethereum Detail: Ethereum launched in July 2015 just before NIST finalized FIPS 202 (August 2015). NIST changed the domain-separation padding byte from 0x01 (original Keccak-256) to 0x06 (standardized SHA3-256). Therefore, Ethereum's keccak256() produces different hashes than standard NIST SHA3-256 for the exact same input!

C. Why use a multi-stage pipeline instead of one single algorithm?

No single mathematical primitive can simultaneously be human-memorizable, tree-derivable, capable of digital signatures, and compact:

  • BIP-39 / BIP-32 / BIP-44 (Key Management & Derivation): Hash functions cannot sign transactions, and elliptic curves are slow for key stretching. HMAC-SHA512 / PBKDF2 safely turn human words into a structured tree of keys.
  • secp256k1 (Asymmetric Trapdoor & Signatures): We need algebraic homomorphism (Q = kG) so a user can sign transactions with k and smart contracts/nodes can verify them with Q (via ECDSA).
  • Keccak-256 (Hashing & Compression): Shortens a 64-byte public key to a 20-byte address (saving Ethereum storage/gas) and destroys the algebraic curve structure until the first outgoing transaction is signed.
  • EIP-55 (Checksum & Error Detection): Raw hex addresses have no error checking. EIP-55 embeds a 15-bit-equivalent checksum directly into the capitalization of letters without changing the 40-character address length.

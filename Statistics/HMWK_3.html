HMWK 2 — Penetration Scores: Law of Large Numbers & Central Limit Theorem

Model: n systems, m attackers. Attacker j scores X₁, X₂, …, Xₙ (i.i.d.) on the n systems. Bernoulli: Xᵢ ∈ {0,1}, P(Xᵢ=1)=p. Rademacher: Xᵢ ∈ {−1,+1}, P(±1)=½.

1. Simulation Setup

Theory: E[Sₙ]=nμ, Var(Sₙ)=nσ², Var(Sₙ/n)=σ²/n, Var(Sₙ/√n)=σ². Bernoulli: μ=p, σ²=p(1−p). Rademacher: μ=0, σ²=1.

3. Interpretation

  • Meaning of n and m. Each of the n systems gives an attacker one chance to score. Sₙ is the attacker’s total penetration score (Bernoulli: number of systems breached; Rademacher: breaches minus repelled attempts). The m attackers are independent replications, i.e. the m trajectories.
  • Law of Large Numbers. Sₙ/n converges in probability to μ (p for Bernoulli, 0 for Rademacher). Chebyshev gives P(|Sₙ/n − μ| > ε) ≤ σ²/(nε²). The empirical curves in the LLN plots fall to 0 and stay below this bound: in a large system, an attacker’s average success rate becomes predictable.
  • Variance under the three normalizations. Var(Sₙ) = nσ² grows without bound, Var(Sₙ/n) = σ²/n shrinks to 0, and only Var(Sₙ/√n) = σ² stays constant. √n is therefore the scale of typical fluctuations of the total score around its mean, which the table confirms (empirical vs. theoretical).
  • Central Limit Theorem. In the Rademacher case μ=0 and σ=1, so Sₙ/√n → N(0,1) in distribution. The histograms approach the normal density, the Q–Q plot approaches the diagonal and the Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance decreases with n. For Bernoulli with p≠0 the mean of Sₙ/√n equals √n·p and diverges; centering is required, and the standardized score (Sₙ − np)/√(np(1−p)) is the one that converges to N(0,1).
  • System size n. A larger n makes the average penetration rate more precise (spread ∝ 1/√n) while the absolute spread of the total score grows (∝ √n). Large systems are predictable in rate but not in absolute count.
  • Number of attackers m. m does not change the distribution of one attacker’s score. It improves the accuracy of the simulated distributions (Monte Carlo error ∝ 1/√m) and scales the aggregate: the sum over all attackers has mean mnμ and variance mnσ². The aggregate Rademacher score stays centered at 0 with spread √(mn).

3. Interpretation

Independence: X and F are independent when knowing X tells us nothing about F: P(X=x, F=f) = P(X=x)·P(F=f) for every cell, equivalently P(F=f | X=x) = P(F=f). In the table, every conditional row would then look like the marginal row. The χ² test measures how far the observed counts are from the counts this rule would predict, with df = (r − 1)(c − 1). Reject H₀ when χ² exceeds the critical value, or equivalently when p < α.

Conclusion for this dataset

Association is not causation

  • A significant χ² only says the variables are statistically associated: the pattern is unlikely under independence. It does not say which variable drives the other, or whether either does.
  • Confounding: a third variable can create the link. Ice-cream sales and drownings both rise with hot weather, so neither causes the other.
  • Reverse causation and selection effects can also produce association; establishing cause needs a randomized experiment or a careful causal design.
  • Size vs. significance: with large n, tiny effects become “significant”, so report an effect size (Cramér’s V) as well. Not rejecting H₀ is also not proof of independence, only a lack of evidence against it.
